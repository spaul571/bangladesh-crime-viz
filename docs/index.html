Bangladesh Police crime statistics · January 2019 – August 2026

Fewer cases, more crime

Every monthly crime table Bangladesh Police Headquarters published from January 2019 to August 2026, digitised and merged into one dataset: 1,497,474 cases in 92 months across 17 police units. Recorded cases fell after 5 August 2024, yet the crimes victims report rose. Start with the dashboard below for quick access to the data, then read the story. Chapter 1 · What is counted, and how much?

A country in numbers

Most of what the police count is not crime that someone reported. It is crime the police went looking for.

The 1,497,474 cases fall into two families of almost equal size. Reported crime (886,992 cases) is led by the catch-all “other cases” and by woman and child repression, which alone is 18.2% of reported crime. Recovery cases (610,482, 40.8% of the total) are police-initiated seizures; narcotics alone are 38.3% of everything recorded. Murder, the crime that dominates headlines, is 26,143 cases, 1.7% of the total. Click a ring to zoom into a family.

Because the two families move in opposite directions, the national total misleads on its own. Reported crime (orange) has a yearly rhythm and a gentle upward drift; recovery cases (aqua) fell by about half. Their sum (violet) peaked at 20,955 in May 2019 and inherits the decline of the second family. Two months stand out: April 2020 (9,098 cases), when the COVID-19 shutdown emptied the streets, and August 2024 (8,833), the lowest in the series.

Why it matters

A headline saying “recorded crime fell” is technically true and practically misleading. Everything that follows separates reported crime from police-initiated recovery cases.

Chapter 2 · Is there a normal year?

The rhythm of an ordinary year

Crime in Bangladesh has a calendar: it climbs from February to a peak in May and June and falls to its lowest point in December.

Reported crime peaks in May and bottoms out in December, a swing of about 28% between the strongest and weakest months; its STL seasonal strength is 0.55 on a 0–1 scale. Switch the heatmap to recovery cases and the rhythm disappears, while the outlined August 2024 cell turns pale.

Each crime has its own season. Woman and child repression rises from March and stays high until October; murder peaks in July; burglary and theft peak in August and September, the lean season before the main rice harvest. The averages use normal years only, so the shocks of 2020 and 2024 do not distort them; switch to all years to see how much those shocks move the profile.

The STL decomposition separates trend, season and shock. Pick any series to see its trend step after August 2024 and the residual shocks of the lockdown months.

Which crimes move together? Burglary, robbery, dacoity, kidnapping and theft rise and fall together. Narcotics, smuggling and arms recoveries form a separate block that correlates negatively with robbery and dacoity: months with more police searches tend to be months with less violent property crime. Compare the periods before and after the break to see how the blocks changed.

Chapter 3 · 2020

The pandemic pause

The lockdown showed what happens when policing and public life stop at the same time: almost everything recorded falls.

During the general holiday that began on 26 March 2020, recorded cases fell to 9,098 in April 2020. Recovery cases fell furthest, to 1,808, because police were redeployed to enforce the lockdown. By July 2020 the series had recovered (Figure 3). 2020 is a useful control: a collapse in recorded crime followed by a quick rebound is what a temporary pause looks like. August 2024 looks different.

Chapter 4 · What changed, by how much, and for how long?

Rupture: after the fifth of August

In July 2024 the protests appeared in the data as riots and assaults on police. In August, the recording system itself broke. When it came back, the country's crime mix had changed.

July 2024 recorded 52 riot cases and 170 police-assault cases, both the highest in the series. Then, after 5 August, recorded cases fell 41.0% in one month, to 8,833. Narcotics recovery fell from 4,852 to 966 cases, yet murder cases jumped to 626, the highest monthly figure on record. Each panel below shows one crime head with its 24-month mean before and after the break.

The before-and-after ledger adds uncertainty. Kidnapping more than doubled (+111.5%), from 41 to 87 cases a month; its Cliff's δ of 1.00 means every one of the 24 months after the break exceeded every one of the 24 months before. Dacoity rose +84.7% and robbery +48.4%. Arms Act cases rose +29.4%; narcotics fell −25.3%.

Was it the break, or the trend? Segmented negative-binomial models control for the pre-existing trend, the season and the COVID months. They still find large immediate level changes: ×2.17 for kidnapping (95% CI 1.84–2.54), ×2.32 for dacoity (1.99–2.71) and ×1.36 for robbery (1.21–1.52). For narcotics the level dropped to ×0.58.

Two unsupervised methods agree on the timing without being told where to look. PELT places change points for dacoity in September 2024, kidnapping in September 2024 and robbery in October 2024. Isolation Forest flags Apr 2020, Aug 2024, May 2020, Sep 2024, May 2019, Feb 2019 as the most unusual months.

Murder needs care. Footnotes on the scans say 158 of August 2024's 626 murder cases were for killings between 2009 and July 2024 that families had not been able to file; similar notes appear every month from February to September 2025, 791 backlog cases in all. Remove them and the picture changes: raw murder is up +15.0%, but murder excluding backlog filings is up only +4.7% (Mann–Whitney p = 0.18), with a model level change of ×0.99 (95% CI 0.92–1.08).

Violent property crime moved to a new normal. From 2019 to 2023 the median month saw between 146 and 172 dacoity, robbery and kidnapping cases; in 2025 the median was 314.

Chapter 4 in one sentence

After 5 August 2024 Bangladesh recorded fewer cases overall because police searched and seized less, while victims reported far more dacoity, robbery and kidnapping; the apparent murder surge is mostly delayed filings.

Chapter 5 · Where did it happen?

The geography of the break

The rise in violent crime reached every part of the country, but not equally.

Police units are grouped into the eight census divisions (each metro unit with its range) and divided by population. In 2025 reported crime per person was highest in Barishal (109 per 100,000) and lowest in Mymensingh (58) and Chattogram (64). The change in violent crime is a different map: +68% in Barishal, +59% in Khulna, +51% in Dhaka, against +11% in Mymensingh, +17% in Rangpur.

16 of the 17 police units recorded more violent crime after the break; the exception is CMP (+0%, essentially flat). In relative terms the biggest rises were KMP (+138%), BMP (+115%), DMP (+108%).

Ranked by reported crime, the large district ranges lead. Chittagong Range and Dhaka Range hold the top two places in every year. Hover a line to follow one unit; the animated race below replays the same ranking year by year.

Clustering the units by the mix of their caseload, not its size, gives three groups. The metro and port belt (CMP, Chittagong Range, DMP, KMP, Khulna Range, RMP, SMP) over-indexes on narcotics, arms, robbery and burglary. The northern agrarian ranges (Mymensingh Range, Rajshahi Range, Rangpur Range, Sylhet Range) over-index on smuggling, consistent with their long land border. The Dhaka–Barishal ring (BMP, Barishal Range, Dhaka Range, GMP, RPMP) over-indexes on dacoity and kidnapping. The two PCA axes explain 71% of the variation; a silhouette of 0.26 says these are tendencies rather than sharp borders.

Chapter 6 · Where is it heading?

A slow return?

Searches and seizures are rising again and violent crime is easing, but not yet back to where it was.

Comparing January–August 2026 with the same months of 2025, recovery cases are up 32.3%, driven by narcotics (+33.9%): the strongest sign yet that proactive policing is returning, in the year of the 12 February 2026 general election. At the same time dacoity is down 26.4%, murder down 9.6% and robbery down 7.1%.

Walk-forward backtests over the last 12 observed months beat a seasonal-naive forecast for every series: total cases 8.3% mean absolute percentage error against 10.6%, reported crime 5.5% against 8.5%.

Data and methods

How this was made

Bangladesh Police Headquarters publishes its monthly crime statistics as scanned PDF tables. All 27 files covering January 2019 – August 2026 were downloaded, read with OCR and checked cell by cell against the printed row, column and recovery totals; the monthly tables for 2019–2023 add up exactly to the printed annual summaries. The result is one Excel file with 1,564 rows (92 months × 17 police units), each tagged with the last day of its month (for example 30 Jun 2025). National figures on this site are the sum of the 17 units.

Download the dataset (Excel)

  • Before and after: 24 months before (Aug 2022 – Jul 2024) against 24 months after (Sep 2024 – Aug 2026); August 2024 excluded. Mann–Whitney U, Cliff's δ, bootstrap 95% CI of the ratio of means.
  • Interrupted time series: negative-binomial GLM with trend, post-break level and slope, month effects and COVID / August-2024 dummies.
  • Seasonality: robust STL (period 12) on log counts.
  • Forecasting: SARIMAX on log counts, AIC-selected orders for d = 0 and d = 1, kept by walk-forward backtest.
  • Change points and anomalies: PELT (RBF cost, penalty 4); Isolation Forest (500 trees, contamination 6%).
  • Clustering: k-means (k = 3) on the centred log-ratio of each unit's crime mix; Railway Range left out as a one-unit outlier.
  • Caveat: these are cases recorded by police, not all crimes committed. Recording depends on victims coming forward and on police capacity.

Charts are drawn with Plotly and Vega-Altair; the dashboard at the top is the web version of the Dash dashboard. Charts that the printed report drew with matplotlib and seaborn are redrawn here with Plotly, because static images cannot be interactive. Every chart has its data as a CSV link below it.
